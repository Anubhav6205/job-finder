An early-career business analyst or product owner who turns business needs into clear, testable requirements and a well-ordered backlog. At 23 years, they should run discovery for a feature or small product area on their own, write stories the team can build without constant clarification, and accept work against agreed criteria, with a senior product manager or architect guiding priorities and scope.

They will work on our internal AI and agent-based applications and on customer projects, so they must be comfortable learning a domain quickly and working closely with engineers. We prefer candidates with domain knowledge in marketing, finance, or risk and compliance.

What separates this level from entry level: they ask the right questions without a script, spot gaps and edge cases before development starts, and can say no to scope that does not serve the goal.

**Experience requirements**

* 2–3 years as a business analyst, product owner, associate product manager or similar role on software projects.
* Has written requirements or user stories for at least one feature or product that went live.
* Has worked in an agile team with engineers, testers and designers through full sprints and releases.
* Has run workshops or interviews with business stakeholders and documented the outcomes.
* Has supported user acceptance testing and a go-live.
* Degree in business, information systems, computer science or related field, or equivalent experience.
* A certification is welcome but not required (e.g. ECBA / CCBA, PSPO or CSPO).

**Nice to have**

* Worked on an AI, chatbot or automation product, or written requirements for one.
* Preferred: domain knowledge in marketing, finance, or risk and compliance (e.g. campaign and lead processes, budgeting and financial reporting, regulatory controls, audit and policy management).
* Contract management, procurement or legal domain knowledge.
* Integration projects: field mapping, interface specifications, test data preparation.
* B2B SaaS experience with enterprise customers.

**Working style**

* Curious: asks “why” until the real need is clear.
* Structured: keeps documents, backlog and decisions organized and current.
* Writes clearly and briefly; uses examples and diagrams instead of long text.
* Collaborative with engineers; respects their estimates and constraints.
* Owns follow-ups and closes loops with stakeholders.
